    - **The operator-impact narrative**: an operator configures Slack notifications with their Slack webhook URL. Slack's webhook endpoint experiences a brief outage (15-minute degradation). The platform's `AlertNotificationMessageProcessor` (per sibling sidecar) iterates `senders` serially. The Slack send blocks for 75-120 seconds (JDK socket timeout default). During those 75-120 seconds: (a) the notification-subscriber thread is held; (b) other alerts queued behind this one wait; (c) the email channel (sibling sender) waits for its turn; (d) the webhook channel also waits. After 75-120s the JDK socket timeout fires; the Slack send fails; the loop continues. Operators see "alerts delayed by 2+ minutes" during Slack outages with no doc-side warning.
    - **The compound with DOC-GAP-220 (case-sensitivity trap)**: the SMTP case-sensitivity trap (DOC-GAP-220) silently disables STARTTLS, which on STARTTLS-required relays causes connection failure. Combined with the SMTP-timeouts-unset finding, an operator on a STARTTLS-required relay hits a hang-indefinitely failure mode. The two findings together = full email-pipeline freeze.
    - **The single-thread bottleneck compound** (cross-sidecar evidence at sibling NotificationSubscriber): "the subscriber is single-threaded per sibling NotificationSubscriber.java". A hung Slack endpoint blocks ALL notifications via the single subscriber thread. The serial fan-out + single subscriber means the platform's outbound notification throughput is constrained by the SLOWEST channel.
    - **The cross-channel-bulkhead absence**: the platform shares ONE `HttpClient` across Slack + Webhook senders. A per-channel HttpClient with bulkhead-isolated executors would prevent cross-channel hang propagation. Per the sidecar's `performance.known_performance_gaps.[2]`: "wiring decision committed here by using one HttpClient + one bean topology rather than per-channel clients with bulkheads".
