  - **Decision statement**: The Notifications WAL consumer is a cluster-wide singleton coordinated by Postgres advisory lock. At application startup (`ApplicationReadyEvent`), `NotificationSubscriberStarter.java:21-32` creates a single-thread `ExecutorService` named `notification-subscriber-thread` and submits the subscriber loop. The loop calls `leaderElectionManager.acquire(walProperties.getAdvisoryLockId(), true)` with the configurable `notifications.wal.advisory-lock-id` (default 100, distinct from the partition manager's 90, DataCollaboration receiver's 110, and DataCollaboration sender's 120). The `true` flag blocks until leadership is acquired; non-leader replicas remain blocked. Only the leader replica drains the WAL via `NotificationSubscriber.java:39-46`'s replication-stream loop; other replicas wait. The decision codifies three structural properties: (a) exactly-once-cluster-wide WAL consumption — duplicate notifications are not produced even in HA deployments; (b) Postgres-as-only-dependency — no Redis/Kafka/Consul broker required for leader election; (c) failover via Postgres advisory-lock-release semantics — on JVM kill, the lock is released and another replica acquires it. The pattern parallels ADR-CANDIDATE-020 (DataCollaboration single-leader sender for outbound Slack); together they form ODD's "single-leader-via-Postgres-advisory-lock" project convention for cross-instance coordination.
  - **Wisdom test**: PASS. The single-thread executor + named thread + blocking-acquire pattern is the intent anchor; the alternative (multi-thread consumer with per-message coordination, or external broker) was explicitly NOT chosen. Structural — every future cluster-wide-singleton service should reuse this pattern with a distinct advisory-lock-id.
