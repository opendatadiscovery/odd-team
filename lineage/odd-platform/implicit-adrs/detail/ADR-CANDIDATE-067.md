## ADR-CANDIDATE-067 — `@ReactiveTransactional` boundary asymmetry — list-shaped reads stay OUTSIDE TX; per-resource writes ARE INSIDE TX (multi-step)

**Severity**: MEDIUM
**Classification**: promote
**Support count**: 14 sidecars (4 controller-method + 5 repository + 5 service — batch G + batch H + batch I)
**Axes present**: controllers, services, repositories

**Surfaced by**:

- `addDataEntityTerm.md:implicit_adrs[0]` (service-level `@ReactiveTransactional` on writes, controller-level absence)
- `upsertDataEntityInternalDescription.md:implicit_adrs[6]` (nested `@ReactiveTransactional` on outer + inner service)

This allows individual repository methods to compose freely under either a shared transaction (when a caller wraps several calls in @ReactiveTransactional) or no transaction (when a caller fires-and-forgets).   - `ReactiveLineageRepositoryImpl.md:invariants[6]` (no @ReactiveTransactional at class or method level; the paired `batchDeleteByEstablisherOddrn` + `batchInsertLineages` rely on caller's `@ReactiveTransactional` at `LineageServiceImpl.java:124-133`)

  - `DataEntityServiceImpl.md:implicit_adrs[0]` ("`@ReactiveTransactional` boundaries are placed at the service tier, NOT the repository tier… 8 occurrences at lines 197/246/288/308/324/337/357/388/411 + `ReactiveDataEntityRepositoryImpl.java:88-91` (no @Transactional / @ReactiveTransactional on the repository class) + `DataEntityInternalStateServiceImpl.java:55/75/101` (the deeper tier follows the same pattern)" — 8 @ReactiveTransactional annotations on ONE service class is the strongest single-service confirmation of the placement)
  - `IngestionService.md:implicit_adrs[0]` ("Single-transaction-per-batch is intentional — the `@ReactiveTransactional` annotation at IngestionServiceImpl.java:66 wraps the entire 3-phase processor chain inside ONE transaction.   - `PolicyServiceImpl.md:implicit_adrs[4]` (the NEGATIVE case — `PolicyServiceImpl` is deliberately non-transactional, asymmetric vs `RoleServiceImpl` which IS transactional; the inconsistency is itself a finding — confidence LOW per the sidecar; routed under bugs_limitations_corner_cases as well — see REFACTOR-266 lost-update race)
  - `LineageServiceImpl.md:implicit_adrs[1]` ("Establisher-keyed atomic-rewrite contract for ingestion — replaceLineagePaths takes a `List<LineagePojo>`, extracts the SET of establishers, deletes ALL edges by those establishers, then inserts the supplied pojos, ALL inside a single @ReactiveTransactional." — confirms ingestion-atomicity via service-tier transactional composition)

**Decision statement**: The platform applies `@ReactiveTransactional` selectively: per-resource write paths (description, internal_name, terms, tags, ownership, status, attachment, metadata, alert ingestion, lineage rewrite-by-establisher, policy CRUD bulk paths) carry `@ReactiveTransactional` at the SERVICE LAYER — never at the controller, **never at the repository**. Multi-step writes (write + FTS-vector refresh + filled-flag toggle + activity-log emission + cross-service side effects) span ONE transaction. Conversely, list-shaped reads (`listPopular`, `listAssociated`, `listByOwner`, `listAllWithStatusOpen`, `getLineageRelations`) carry NO `@ReactiveTransactional` — they are single SELECTs with no side effects. `getDataEntityDetails` (batch F) is the deliberate exception on the read side: it carries `@ReactiveTransactional` BECAUSE of the read-as-write view_count UPDATE (a deliberate read-write coupling). The asymmetry is STRUCTURAL: the TX boundary is a function of whether the operation mutates state, not a uniform pattern.

**Batch H deepening**: The repository-layer is **uniformly transaction-free** across ALL 5 repositories examined. Not a single `@ReactiveTransactional` annotation at the repository class level. Single-statement INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE methods are atomic per-statement; multi-statement consistency depends on the caller's transaction boundary holding the DB connection for the duration. The deliberate-non-transactionality at the repository is its own architectural commitment: it allows repository methods to compose freely either under a shared transaction (typical case) or as fire-and-forget (test code, scheduled jobs). The trade-off is that any caller bypassing the service layer (a future direct controller-to-repository call, a one-off script) silently loses atomicity. This is documented in `ReactiveDataEntityRepositoryImpl.md:implicit_adrs[5]` as "the trade-off is that callers can forget to wrap and silently lose atomicity" — the maintainer accepts this risk.

**Batch I deepening (service-tier primary source)**: The 5 service-layer sidecars confirm the pattern at the placement layer:
- **DataEntityServiceImpl is the strongest single-class confirmation**: 8 `@ReactiveTransactional` annotations at lines 197/246/288/308/324/337/357/388/411, ZERO at the controller, ZERO at the repository. The annotation placement is the maintainer's choice repeated 8 times.
- **IngestionService confirms the single-transaction-per-batch decision**: the entire 3-phase processor chain (per ADR-CANDIDATE-079) runs inside ONE `@ReactiveTransactional`. The annotation is at the orchestration entry point.
- **AlertServiceImpl confirms the asymmetric-per-method pattern**: 2 of 10 methods carry the annotation explicitly (the bulk-ingestion paths); 1 delegates to the AOP synthetic transaction (the user-driven mutation path); 7 are read paths without transaction. The asymmetry is structural.
- **LineageServiceImpl confirms the ingestion-atomicity composition**: the `replaceLineagePaths` annotation composes with the repository's establisher-keyed schema (per ADR-CANDIDATE-072) to provide atomic per-establisher rewrite.
- **PolicyServiceImpl surfaces the NEGATIVE case at service tier**: PolicyServiceImpl's create/update/delete are non-transactional in contrast to sibling RoleServiceImpl's transactional versions. The inconsistency-with-sibling-service is an operator-visible consequence (lost-update race at PolicyServiceImpl.java:71-81 — see REFACTOR-266). The pattern is "service-tier transactional placement is opt-IN per service; the maintainer chooses which service-tier needs the boundary."

The full triangulation: (a) controllers carry NO @ReactiveTransactional (18 controller sidecars confirm); (b) services carry @ReactiveTransactional on per-resource write paths and selective methods (5 service sidecars confirm placement); (c) repositories carry NO @ReactiveTransactional (5 repository sidecars confirm uniform non-transactionality). The three-axis pattern is the architectural commit.

**Evidence**:
- `getPopular.md` says: "No transactional boundary on the read path... Contrast with `getDataEntityDetails` (batch F implicit_adrs[2]) which carries `@ReactiveTransactional` to wrap the read + the view-count UPDATE in one transaction. `listPopular` reads `view_count` but never writes — the producer/consumer asymmetry is the implicit decision: the consumer half does not amplify the loop."
- `upsertDataEntityInternalDescription.md` says: "nested transactional annotations on both layers reflect the intent that a partial-failure state (e.g. description written but term-relations not updated) is forbidden."
- `createDataEntityTagsRelations.md` says: "Reactive transactional boundary is duplicated — both `DataEntityServiceImpl.upsertTags` and `TagServiceImpl.updateRelationsWithDataEntity` carry `@ReactiveTransactional`. The outer-tx encloses the inner one (Spring's default `PROPAGATION_REQUIRED`)."
- (NEW 2026-05-19H) `ReactiveLineageRepositoryImpl.md` says: "Ingestion atomicity via paired `batchDeleteByEstablisherOddrn` + `batchInsertLineages` under caller's `@ReactiveTransactional` — the repository deliberately exposes the delete-by-establisher and insert primitives as separate methods rather than a single `replaceLineagesByEstablisher(...)` so the caller (LineageServiceImpl.replaceLineagePaths at LineageServiceImpl.java:124-133) can compose them inside ONE transactional boundary."
- (NEW 2026-05-19H) `ReactiveAlertRepositoryImpl.md` says: "Repository layer is transaction-free; transactions are declared at the SERVICE layer."
- (NEW 2026-05-19I) `DataEntityServiceImpl.md` says: "8 @ReactiveTransactional occurrences at lines 197/246/288/308/324/337/357/388/411 — the pattern is recognised idiom, reproduced by every reactive service in the project."
- (NEW 2026-05-19I) `IngestionService.md` says: "The `@ReactiveTransactional` annotation IS the decision; the maintainer chose a custom annotation (vs raw `@Transactional`) to make the boundary visible. The contract is binary (all-or-nothing) by design."
- (NEW 2026-05-19I) `PolicyServiceImpl.md` says (the negative case): "Service composition methods (create, update, delete) are NON-TRANSACTIONAL at this layer, in deliberate inconsistency with the sibling RoleServiceImpl which IS transactional… WEAK — there is no comment, no exception message, no naming convention defending the absence. The OPERATOR-VISIBLE consequence is the lost-update race."

**Rationale (wisdom test 3-question)**:
1. *Intentional?* YES — uniform pattern: writes carry the annotation at the service, reads do not, except where the read mutates (getDataEntityDetails). The pattern is consistent across 14 sidecars now (4 controller + 5 repository + 5 service). Defensive duplicate-annotation on nested services (Tags, Description) is intentional. The strongest single-class evidence is DataEntityServiceImpl's 8 annotations on one class.
2. *Structural impact?* YES — affects connection-pool usage, partial-failure semantics, isolation level inheritance, the rule "controllers are TX-naive", and the rule "repositories are TX-naive" (so they compose under any caller). The selective service-layer placement IS the architecture.
3. *Refactoring or structural?* STRUCTURAL — switching to "all paths transactional" or "all paths non-transactional" would be a redesign. Switching to "transactions live at the repository" would also be a redesign (it would break the composability that the service-layer boundary enables).
→ ADR-CANDIDATE.

**Existing ADR**: partial overlap with ADR-CANDIDATE-059 (which says "Multi-step per-data-entity write paths use service-layer @ReactiveTransactional").
