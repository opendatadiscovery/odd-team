## REFACTOR-331 — Write-collaborative DEG membership: `DATA_ENTITY_ADD_TO_GROUP` scopes on the CHILD entity, not the parent DEG — any holder can pollute any manually-created DEG with arbitrary entities; cross-owner DEG-membership writes are permitted by design

**Severity**: HIGH
**Category**: missing-auth (resource-scope-asymmetry between Policy gate and architectural intent)
**Pillars affected**: [P-09-security-access-control, P-01-data-discovery, P-08-management-administration]
**Batch**: L (2026-05-19)

**Surfaced by**:

The target `data_entity_group_id` is in the BODY and has NO authorization check. A caller with `DATA_ENTITY_ADD_TO_GROUP` on entity X can therefore add X into ANY manually-created DEG in the catalog — including DEGs they do not own, DEGs in a different organizational silo, the Administrators-only 'Critical Systems' domain, etc. The DEG operator who curates 'Finance Domain' has no veto on what entities end up in their domain"
- `odd-platform__java__DataEntityController__controller-method__addDataEntityDataEntityGroup.md:security.known_security_gaps.[0]` (HIGH)
- `odd-platform__java__DataEntityController__controller-method__deleteDataEntityFromDataEntityGroup.md:security.known_security_gaps.[1]` (LOW for the delete side — symmetric: the gate is on the data-entity, not the DEG; a DEG owner cannot remove a stale entity from their own DEG unless they ALSO own the entity)

**Description**: The `SecurityRule` at `SecurityConstants.java:228-231` registers `POST /api/dataentities/{data_entity_id}/data_entity_group` with `AuthorizationManagerType.DATA_ENTITY` and `DATA_ENTITY_ADD_TO_GROUP`. The `data_entity_id` from the PATH is the resource-scope basis; `URLResourceExtractor` pulls it, `DataEntityPermissionExtractor` evaluates the caller's Policies against THAT child entity's resolver context. The target DEG id (`data_entity_group_id`) lives in the REQUEST BODY and has NO authorization check anywhere in the controller→service→repository chain. A caller with `DATA_ENTITY_ADD_TO_GROUP` on entity X (typically because the caller's Owner is on the entity's owner list AND the Policy uses the `"is": "dataEntity:owner"` scope) can therefore place X into ANY manually-created DEG in the catalog — including DEGs they do not own, DEGs in a different team's Domain, the Administrators-only 'Critical Systems' DEG, etc. The DEG operator who curates 'Finance Domain' or 'Critical Systems' has no veto on what entities end up in their DEG.

The cross-pillar implication for **P-01 Data Discovery**: the Catalog Overview's Domain tiles can be polluted from the outside — a careless authorised caller can drop unrelated entities into a Domain whose owner had no input. The cross-pillar implication for **P-09 Security & Access Control**: the read-collaborative posture (every authenticated user sees all entities per ADR-CANDIDATE-003) combines with the no-DEG-side-gate to produce a **write-collaborative DEG surface** — every `DATA_ENTITY_ADD_TO_GROUP`-holder can write into any DEG. Operators cannot defend the DEG from drive-by membership writes without revoking `DATA_ENTITY_ADD_TO_GROUP` from every owner. The cross-pillar implication for **P-08 Management**: there is no `DATA_ENTITY_GROUP_MEMBERSHIP_MANAGE`-style DEG-side permission today; the Owner of a DEG cannot defend their DEG.

Combined with **REFACTOR-332** (forensic silence — no activity event on membership flips), the consequence is the worst-case shape: drive-by DEG pollution that the DEG owner cannot prevent AND cannot forensically reconstruct. The cross-link is: REFACTOR-331 is the security architecture gap; REFACTOR-332 is the audit gap; together they form a write-collaborative-DEG-without-audit cluster.

**Existing-ADR-or-implied-prescription**: **ADR-CANDIDATE-002** (centralised SECURITY_RULES) documents the architecture — Policy permissions are wired via SECURITY_RULES with a resource-scope type. **ADR-CANDIDATE-113 (NEW batch L)** documents the manually-created discipline (DEGs are first-class data entities); the discipline is defended at the DEG IDENTITY (DEG must exist and be manually-created) but NOT at the DEG-OWNER-SCOPE level. The implied prescription is that DEG-side ownership SHOULD govern membership writes; the code does not realise it. The live Permissions doc text "Allows adding a data entity to a manually created group" is misleading — a reasonable operator reading the docs would assume DEG-side scoping; the code's child-side scoping is undocumented.

**Proposed remedy**: Three options. **(a) Add DEG-side permission gating**: introduce a `DATA_ENTITY_GROUP_MANAGE_MEMBERSHIP` permission scoped on `DATA_ENTITY_GROUP` (the parent DEG); register a SecurityRule that requires it for the membership endpoints; the `DataEntityServiceImpl.addDataEntityToDEG` reads `formData.getDataEntityGroupId()` and performs `permissionService.hasPermission(MANAGE_MEMBERSHIP, dataEntityGroupId)` at the service layer (defence-in-depth). **(b) Require both child-side AND DEG-side permissions**: keep `DATA_ENTITY_ADD_TO_GROUP` on the child + add the DEG-side permission as a compound requirement. **(c) Accept the model and document it explicitly**: update the live Permissions page + groups-domains.md to call out the cross-owner addability as intentional; operators authoring Policies see the model clearly. Option (a) or (b) is the structural fix; option (c) closes the doc-drift without changing behaviour. The maintainer's triage is whether DEG-side ownership should be a Policy concept (current platform doesn't model "DEG ownership" as distinct from "the DEG's data-entity-row owner list" — the question reduces to whether DEG-side ownership is meaningfully different from data-entity-side ownership when the DEG IS a data entity).

**Severity rationale**: HIGH — write-collaborative DEG surface combined with the live Permissions doc that does not disambiguate the scope; an operator-trap that affects every Policy authoring decision involving DEG membership. Pairs with REFACTOR-332 (audit gap) for the compound impact. Under `auth.type=DISABLED`, the consequence is anonymous DEG pollution with no audit trail.

**Suggested backlog grouping**: `Authorization audit batch` (paired with REFACTOR-332, REFACTOR-217 — the term-management path-mismatch fixes the cross-owner ADD_TERM, this scope fixes the cross-owner ADD_TO_GROUP analogously)
