## Description

The Business Glossary "Security and RBAC bypasses" section carries a warning hint about term creation draining pre-existing `[[namespace:term]]` mentions into real links. The sentence states: *"The platform maintains **two staging tables** for `[[namespace:term]]` mentions that referenced a term that did not yet exist; the moment the term is created, the staging tables drain and the auto-links are written."*

There are **three** such `*_unhandled_term` staging tables, not two:
1. `data_entity_description_unhandled_term` — created in `V0_0_78__description_unhandled_term.sql:1`. Stages term mentions in **data-entity descriptions**.
2. `dataset_field_description_unhandled_term` — created in `V0_0_78__description_unhandled_term.sql:13`. Stages term mentions in **dataset-field (column) descriptions**.
3. `term_definition_unhandled_term` — created later, in `V0_0_91__add_term_to_term.sql:14`. Stages term mentions in **another term's definition** (the term-to-term description-link path introduced alongside the `term_to_term` table).

The undercount understates the blast radius of the very compliance caveat the hint is making: the warning's point is that creating a popular term name (`Customer`, `Order`, `PII`) triggers a burst of auto-links whose authorship cannot be reconstructed from platform logs. That burst materialises from all three drains — data-entity-description mentions, column-description mentions, **and** term-definition mentions — so an operator reading "two staging tables" underestimates both the surfaces affected (term-to-term definition links are missing from their mental model) and the scope of the audit gap.
