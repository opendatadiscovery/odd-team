## Description

`business-glossary.md` contradicts itself on whether term ownership is an *enforced authority* or an *editorial responsibility*:

- **"Ownership and privileges" (line 50):** "The owner holds the authority to create, approve edits, and delete the associated term, contributing to the relevance of those descriptions."
- **"Ownership and privileges" inside Term-to-entity associations (line 92) — a near-verbatim duplicate:** "This owner holds the authority to create, approve edits, and delete the associated entity, contributing to the relevance of those descriptions."

Both sentences read as if ownership is the gate that controls who may create / edit / delete a term and its links. But the page's own **Security and RBAC bypasses** caveats (lines 141-167) establish the opposite: term-to-entity link/unlink, direct term-to-term link/unlink, namespace creation, tag creation, and the description-mention auto-link side-channel are **NOT gated by ownership** (or in several cases by any `TERM_*` permission) — any authenticated user can mutate them. Create/delete of the term itself is gated by `TERM_CREATE` / `TERM_DELETE` **permissions** (line 52-62 table), held via Policy grants — not by being the term's owner.

An operator authoring deployment-time RBAC reads line 50, assumes assigning an owner confers an enforced create/approve/delete gate on that term, and builds a separation-of-duties policy on a guarantee the platform does not provide. The ownership sentence and the caveats sit in the same document with no in-text reconciliation; the reader has to notice the contradiction themselves. The line-92 copy compounds the problem — it duplicates the misleading claim and additionally says "delete the associated **entity**" (the data entity), which overstates the owner's reach further.

**The contradiction, on the page itself:**
- Authority claim — `business-glossary.md:50` and the duplicate at `:92`.
- Refuting caveats — `business-glossary.md:141-167` (Security and RBAC bypasses):
  - `:141` term-to-entity link/unlink "Any authenticated user … can link or unlink any term to any data entity regardless of the Policy grants" (path-rename bug `/term` vs `/terms`).
  - `:147` direct term-to-term links "not gated by `TERM_UPDATE` — or by any permission."
  - `:151` description-mention auto-link writes link rows "without consulting `DATA_ENTITY_ADD_TERM`."
  - `:159` / `:163` novel namespace / tag names auto-create without `NAMESPACE_CREATE` / `TAG_CREATE`.

**Canonical permission model (the page's own table, `:52-62`):** the seven `TERM_*` permissions — `TERM_CREATE`, `TERM_UPDATE`, `TERM_DELETE`, `TERM_OWNERSHIP_{CREATE,UPDATE,DELETE}`, `TERM_TAGS_UPDATE` — are the actual gates, granted via Policies (see `../configuration-and-deployment/enable-security/authorization/permissions.md`). Ownership (`TERM_OWNERSHIP_*`) assigns *who is the owner*; it does not gate term create/edit/delete. This is the canonical fact the prose must align to. The RBAC-bypass caveats themselves were validated in prior backlog work (the danger-hint text is already shipped on the live page), so the contradiction is between long-standing intro prose and already-published caveats.

This is a Gate-1 (no-duplicates / intra-page) + Gate-9 (claim provenance) finding: the duplicated authority sentence both repeats itself and contradicts the page's verified caveats.
